> Rhythm is a regulation instrument. It organizes action, anticipation, and collective timing faster than verbal explanation.

## Why this module exists
When temporal design is random, classes feel fragmented. Rhythm architecture gives participants predictable structure and supports timing dependent skill acquisition.

## Core concepts
### Neural entrainment
Periodic sound and movement cues can synchronize attention and motor timing.

### Groove and prediction
High engagement often appears where pattern and surprise are balanced.

### Hypofrontality framing
Absorbed rhythmic effort may reduce self monitoring. This is plausible but should not be overclaimed as universal neuroscience certainty.

### Motor auditory coupling
Auditory timing signals can improve movement consistency in repetitive tasks.

## Design rules
- Establish stable timing before layering complexity.
- Add novelty one variable at a time.
- Use silence as reset, not empty space.
- Align transitions to clear count structures.

## What the evidence actually says
Rhythmic cueing has strong support in motor coordination contexts. Entrainment effects vary by skill level, fatigue, and task complexity.

## Practicum
1. Build three eight count phrase maps.
2. Test with and without music.
3. Insert one intentional silence window.
4. Diagnose timing breakdown points.

## Deliverable
Create a Rhythm Architecture Sheet with phrase maps, transition counts, and adaptation notes.
