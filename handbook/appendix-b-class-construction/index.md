This is the kit you keep open the morning you teach. Use the **One-Page Plan** to design any class in fifteen minutes, the **Default Score** as your fallback arc when you're tired or new to a format, the **Ten Drills** to sharpen craft between classes, and the **Checklist** so nothing operational slips. The **Reflection Card** is how you compound improvement class over class.

> Print it. Tape it to the inside of your binder. Use it for thirty consecutive classes before you start adapting it. Then make it yours.

---

## 1. The One-Page Class Plan

Copy this into a notebook or a fresh document before every class. Fill it in fifteen minutes. If you cannot fill it in fifteen minutes, your intent is not clear yet.

```
CLASS: ___________________   DATE: ___________   LENGTH: ______
ROOM: ___________________   EXPECTED ATTENDANCE: ___________

INTENT (one sentence — what shifts in the room because of this class):
________________________________________________________________

KEY MOVEMENT THEMES (max 3):
1. ____________________________________________________________
2. ____________________________________________________________
3. ____________________________________________________________

ARC (6 phases, with time hack + dominant feeling):
00:00 — ARRIVAL     [feel: _______________________________]

00:00 — PEAK        [feel: _______________________________]

________________________________________________________________

________________________________________________________________

________________________________________________________________
________________________________________________________________

ONE THING I AM PRACTICING TODAY AS A TEACHER:
________________________________________________________________
```

## 2. The Default Score — Six-Phase Arc

When you don't have time to design from scratch, run this. Tune the percentages to class length; the *order* and *intent* do not change.

| Phase | % of class | Intent | Movement quality | Music character | Your voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Arrival** | 5% | Land the body in the room | Stillness, breath, body scan | Sparse, ambient, 60–80 BPM | Quiet, slow, spacious |
| **2. Mobilize** | 15% | Wake fascia, joints, breath | Floor mobility, articulation, gentle range | Building texture, 90–100 BPM | Warm, instructional |
| **3. Build** | 25% | Establish capacity & rhythm | Repetition, simple patterns, finding the groove | Driving, steady, 110–120 BPM | Steady, rhythmic, fewer metaphors |
| **4. Peak** | 25% | Sustained effort, breakthrough | Demanding patterns, intensity, surrender | High energy, 120–140 BPM | Direct, sparse words, more presence |
| **5. Integrate** | 20% | Wring out & soften | Floor work, release, long stretch | Coming down, 90 BPM and falling | Gentle, slower cadence, more silence |
| **6. Close** | 10% | Re-enter the world | Stillness, savasana, seated | Sparse → silence at the end | Almost a whisper, then nothing |

**Worked example — 60-minute class:** 3 / 9 / 15 / 15 / 12 / 6 minutes.
**Worked example — 75-minute class:** 4 / 11 / 19 / 19 / 15 / 7 minutes.
**Worked example — 45-minute class:** 2 / 7 / 11 / 11 / 9 / 5 minutes.

The arc is non-negotiable. The percentages are a starting point. Tune them by feel only after you have run the default at least ten times.

## 3. Ten Teaching Drills

Practice one drill before or after each class. Cycle through all ten across two weeks. Keep a one-line log per drill: what you noticed, what you'll keep, what you'll discard.

1. **The 30-Second Open** — Record yourself delivering your opening words. Play it back at 1×, then 1.5×. Trim every filler word. Re-record. Aim for thirty seconds that could not be cut.
2. **One-Cue Round** — Lead any five minutes giving *only* directional cues. No metaphor, no emotion, no story. Notice what clarity does to the room.
3. **Silent Round** — Lead five minutes using *only* gesture and demonstration. No voice at all. Notice what your body had to say more clearly.
4. **Metaphor Round** — Lead five minutes using *only* image and metaphor cues. No directional language. Notice which images land and which confuse.
5. **The Breath Pace** — Teach a ten-minute sequence breathing audibly at exactly the pace you want students to breathe. Their nervous systems will entrain to yours.
6. **Name Five** — In any class, learn and *use* the names of five students you didn't know before. The room shifts when people are named.
7. **The Cold Read** — Pick a song you have never used. Score one five-minute movement phrase to it on the spot. No re-takes. Notice your defaults.
8. **One Less Word** — Take a familiar sixty-second cue stack. Cut three words. Teach it. Notice what was lost (probably nothing).
9. **The Transition Audit** — Record one class. Listen *only* to the transitions between movements. Find the roughest one. Rewrite it.
10. **Camera On You** — Film yourself teaching from the back of the room. Watch with the sound off. What does your body say to the room?

## 4. Pre-Class Checklist

Print and tape this inside your kit bag. Real teachers run checklists. So do pilots.

### 24 hours before
- [ ] Class plan written on the One-Page template above
- [ ] Music score finalized and downloaded for **offline** play (never trust streaming)
- [ ] Backup playlist queued for tech failure
- [ ] Opening and closing words memorized, not improvised
- [ ] Room, time, and attendance expectations confirmed in writing

### 1 hour before
- [ ] Hydrated; eaten 60–90 minutes prior, not full
- [ ] 10-minute movement warm-up for *yourself*
- [ ] 2 minutes of silent breath before leaving

### 15 minutes before
- [ ] In the room with music tested and volume set
- [ ] Lights, temperature, and props arranged
- [ ] Phone on **Do Not Disturb** (not silent — DND so playback continues)
- [ ] Greet every student who arrives early; use their name if you know it

### 30 seconds before
- [ ] One slow exhale
- [ ] Open with your memorized words — *not* a variation

### Immediately after
- [ ] Thank each student leaving with eye contact and a name where possible
- [ ] Voice memo to yourself: what worked, what didn't, one thing to change next time

## 5. Post-Class Reflection Card

Answer each in **≤2 sentences** within 30 minutes of finishing. Keep these. They become your training data.

1. **Where did the room land** — energized, opened, distracted, flat? Why?
2. **Which phase was strongest? Which was weakest?**
3. **What did I cue that worked? What did I cue that I should retire?**
4. **What is the one thing I am changing for next time?**

After every tenth class, read the last ten reflection cards in one sitting. Patterns will be obvious. Act on them.
